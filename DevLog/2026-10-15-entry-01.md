## What I did

The group was formed at the start of the semester and we split the work by module: black hole mechanics, boss and enemy AI, and level design. I took the boss, which in this game means the military helicopter that closes the final arena.

**Choosing the game.** We wanted a game whose core verb could be built in one semester. *Hole.io* gave us the absorption and growth loop, *Tasty Planet* gave us the progression from a laboratory to a city, and *Destroy All Humans!* gave us the idea of an escalating military response that ends in a boss. The proposal was written around those three references.

**Designing the boss.** I wrote the boss section of the proposal: a gunship deployed once the black hole has consumed enough of the city, fought in two phases (normal and berserk), with a counter-attack loop where the player absorbs the energy the boss drops and throws it back. The loop matters because the player's only ability is absorption — the fight has to reuse it rather than introduce a new verb at the last minute.

**Setting up the project.** Created the Unity project, imported the helicopter model, checked its scale against a one-metre cube, and mapped the parts I would need: the two muzzle points, the four missiles on the pylons and the materials. I also sketched the state machine on paper — Idle, NormalAttack, BerserkMode, Defeated, with the transition between the two attack states driven by remaining health.

## Problems faced

**Scope.** Three levels, environmental puzzles and a boss is far more than two semesters of work. We agreed to treat the boss arena as the vertical slice and develop it first, so that if the schedule slips the fight still stands on its own as a demo.

**Readability on a phone.** A black hole moving across a 3D plane does not leave much screen space, and anything the player cannot read at a glance is unfair. That ruled out a bullet-hell pattern; the boss needed attacks with a clear shape — which is what led to the strafing run in the next period.

**No art budget.** The boss model comes from an existing gunship pack rather than being built for the game, so the muzzle positions and the missile pylons are whatever the model happens to provide. Attack design has to fit the asset instead of the other way round.
